## 1. The "fluent by high school" claim — how to talk about it honestly

The word **fluent** sounds simple but it means different things to different people. To a parent it often means "can have a real conversation." To a linguist it means something more strict.

There is a standard scale used in the United States for language proficiency. It is called the **ACTFL scale** (American Council on the Teaching of Foreign Languages). It has five levels, from lowest to highest:

| Level | What it sounds like in practice |
|---|---|
| **Novice** | Single words, memorized phrases, simple questions and answers on familiar topics |
| **Intermediate** | Full sentences, simple conversations on familiar topics, can describe and narrate in present time |
| **Advanced** | Connected paragraphs, narrate past and future, handle unexpected situations |
| **Superior** | Discuss abstract topics, support opinions with reasoning, near-native control |
| **Distinguished** | Educated native-speaker level |

Each of the first three levels has three sub-levels: **Low**, **Mid**, **High**.

### What is realistic for a K4-Grade 8 program?

The research is clear on this point:

- Children in **full immersion** programs (Spanish is the language of math, science, etc., not just one class) reach **Intermediate** by 5th grade.
- Children in **one Spanish class per week** at an English-medium school reach an average of **Novice-Mid by Grade 8**. ([source: ACTFL research](https://www.actfl.org/research/research-findings/academic-achievement))

LCS is between these two models. We are more focused than one class per week, but we are not full immersion. A realistic target for an LCS student who attends consistently from K4 through Grade 8 is:

- **Novice-High to Intermediate-Low** by the end of Grade 8.

In plain words: by the end of Grade 8, a student can hold a simple conversation in Spanish about familiar topics like family, school, food, hobbies, and weekend plans. They can narrate something that happened to them yesterday. They can ask a question and answer one.

This is **not fluent** by the strict definition. But it is a meaningful achievement and it is honest.

### Recommended way to talk about the LCS promise

Replace "fluent by high school" with something more specific and more defensible. For example:

> *By the end of Grade 8, LCS students hold real conversations in Spanish on the topics they know — family, school, food, travel, daily life. They understand Spanish speakers from many countries. They have the foundation to continue to true fluency in high school or college.*

This is something LCS can actually deliver and defend. It also leaves room for ambitious students — those who take more classes per week — to reach higher.

## 2. Hours of instruction — what the program needs

There is no Wisconsin state law that sets a minimum number of Spanish hours for elementary school. Wisconsin DPI **recommends** starting world language early but does not require it before Grade 7 ([source: WI DPI](https://dpi.wi.gov/world-language)).

ACTFL recommends that instruction be in the target language (Spanish) at least 90% of the time. The typical FLES program (Foreign Language in Elementary School) runs 3 to 5 times per week for about 75 minutes per week total. ([source: FLES brochure](https://www.apsva.us/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/FLES-brochure-2018-2019-1.pdf))

### Proposed LCS hours model

LCS is not a school. We run classes as a separate program, in person or online. So we have flexibility in how we structure hours.

A simple proposed model:

| Track | Classes per week | Class length | Weeks per year | Total hours/year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | 1 | 45 min | 30 | 22.5 |
| **Accelerated** | 2 | 45 min | 30 | 45 |

Across 5 years (K4 → Grade 8 is actually 10 grades total, but a child who starts at K4 and stays through Grade 8 is in the program for **10 years**):

| Track | Total hours K4 → Grade 8 |
|---|---|
| **Standard** (1x/week) | 225 hours |
| **Accelerated** (2x/week) | 450 hours |

For comparison, a full FLES program (5x/week × 75 min, K-8) provides about 405 hours. So:
- **Standard LCS = ~55% of a full FLES program**. Expect end-of-Grade-8 = Novice-High.
- **Accelerated LCS = ~111% of a full FLES program**. Expect end-of-Grade-8 = Intermediate-Low.

## 3. Proficiency targets — what students can do by the end of each grade

This is the most important section of the document. For each grade, here is what an LCS student should be able to do in Spanish by the end of the year. The targets are based on the Standard track (1 class per week). Accelerated track students will move faster.

| Grade | ACTFL Sub-level | What the student can do |
|---|---|---|
| **K4** | Novice-Low (emerging) | Recognize and repeat ~50 high-frequency words. Greet, say their name, name colors, count 1-10. Listen and respond with gestures. |
| **K5** | Novice-Low | Recognize ~100 words. Use simple memorized phrases (*me llamo, tengo X años*). Respond yes/no in Spanish. Sing along to short songs. |
| **Grade 1** | Novice-Low → Mid | Use single-word answers and short memorized phrases. Name 200+ objects. Follow simple classroom directions in Spanish. |
| **Grade 2** | Novice-Mid | Combine memorized chunks into simple statements (*Me gusta el rojo*). Ask basic questions (*¿Cómo te llamas?*). Read short sentences with pictures. |
| **Grade 3** | Novice-Mid → High | Form simple complete sentences in present tense. Describe familiar people and objects. Begin reading short stories. |
| **Grade 4** | Novice-High | Connect 2-3 sentences on familiar topics. Talk about likes, family, school, weekend. Begin past tense recognition. |
| **Grade 5** | Novice-High | Sustain a short exchange on familiar topics. Narrate simple past events with memorized verbs. Read short paragraphs. |
| **Grade 6** | Novice-High → Intermediate-Low | Hold a simple conversation on familiar topics. Ask and answer follow-up questions. Begin writing short paragraphs. |
| **Grade 7** | Intermediate-Low (emerging) | Narrate past events. Describe people, places, and routines in detail. Express opinions on familiar topics. |
| **Grade 8** | Intermediate-Low | Connect ideas across multiple sentences. Compare and contrast. Handle a real conversation with a patient Spanish speaker. |

These targets connect to the **Wisconsin DPI World Languages Standards** (June 2019). The 36 Novice-band indicators are already loaded into the LCS platform. We will map specific indicators to specific grades and units in the WI Standards matrix below.

- **Core vocabulary** (a few sample words to show the level — the full list is larger)
- A **cultural connection** (a Spanish-speaking country or tradition)
- **WI standard codes** (which standards this unit teaches against)

## 5. Vocabulary spiral — when topics return

A good language curriculum **spirals**. Topics introduced in K4 come back in deeper form in later grades. Students do not just memorize and forget; they keep using and extending what they know.

Example spiral for **colors**:

| Grade | What students do with colors |
|---|---|
| K4 | Name 6 basic colors; point to colored objects |
| K5 | Match colors to objects in stories; add 2 more colors |
| Grade 1 | Use colors as adjectives: *la camisa roja, los zapatos negros* (with agreement) |
| Grade 3 | Describe people and places using colors in full sentences |
| Grade 6 | Use colors symbolically in art and music (*amarillo = alegría, azul = tristeza*) |

Example spiral for **family**:

| Grade | What students do with family vocabulary |
|---|---|
| K4 | Name immediate family members |
| K5 | Tell who lives in their house |
| Grade 2 | Describe family members with simple adjectives |
| Grade 4 | Talk about extended family and family traditions |
| Grade 6 | Compare family structures across cultures |
| Grade 8 | Share their own family story with multiple tenses |

This is two examples out of dozens. Karen will likely want to map every major theme across the K4-Grade-8 arc.

Here are the questions where you both need to decide. Some have my recommendation; all are open for change.

1. **Hours per week.** Standard track (1x/week) only, or also offer Accelerated (2x/week)? *My suggestion: start Standard-only and add Accelerated when demand is clear.*

2. **Class format.** Small groups (4-6 students) or larger (8-12)? Different sizes per grade? *My suggestion: small groups for K4-Grade 2 where personal attention matters most; can grow larger for older grades.*

3. **Year length.** 30 weeks (academic year) or longer? Do we offer summer programming? *My suggestion: 30-week core, optional summer enrichment.*

5. **Heritage speakers.** Children whose families speak Spanish at home are at a different starting point. Do we have a separate track? *My suggestion: not for the first year; observe how heritage speakers behave in the mixed class, then decide.*

6. **Cultural country rotation.** The plan above rotates Peru → Mexico → Spain → Argentina → Costa Rica → Colombia → Chile → DR → Guatemala → US. Does this make sense? Karen may want Peru in more than one grade given her own connection. *Open to your preferences.*

7. **Assessment cadence.** Every Unit (5 assessments per year per grade) or just at year-end? Or both — light unit checks + a year-end review? *My suggestion: light unit-end check (already built into the curriculum block system as `:::assess`), plus a year-end review week.*

## How this becomes real on the platform

1. Each grade becomes a **LearningPath** (already in the database — we have K4-2 Foundations seeded as one example).
2. Each unit becomes a **Unit** attached to its grade.
3. Each unit gets its day-by-day lesson plans drafted by the AI drafter (the **Curriculum tab** on the LCS site).
4. Each lesson has its printable artifacts (bingo cards, worksheets, songs, flashcards) — already supported.

The path from this document to a working K4-Grade-8 program is:

1. **You agree on the structure** (or a revised version of it).
2. **Mark loads the LearningPaths and Units** into the database, one row per grade and one row per unit.
3. **You draft lessons** in the Curriculum tab, one Unit at a time. The AI drafter will use your unit theme + WI standards as context.
4. **You review and refine** each draft. Approved lessons become available for teachers to assemble into binders.
