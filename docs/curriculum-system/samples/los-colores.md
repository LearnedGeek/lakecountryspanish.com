:::lesson-overview
**Theme:** Colors — *Los Colores*
**For:** K4–2nd grade Spanish
**Time:** ~25 minutes
**Target vocab:** 6 color words
**Learning goal:** Students can recognize, say, and point to six basic colors in Spanish.
:::

:::warmup {time="3 min"}
🦙 *"¡Hola amigos! Soy Lola la Llama y hoy vamos a aprender los colores."*

Hold up the red card. Ask: *"What color is this in English?"* Then say: *"En español, decimos **rojo**."*

Have everyone repeat: *"Rojo. Rojo. Rojo."*

Do the same for blue (*azul*) and yellow (*amarillo*) — three colors only in the warmup. We'll add the rest in the teach segment.
:::

:::vocab
| Español      | English | Pronunciation        | Cue   |
|--------------|---------|----------------------|-------|
| rojo         | red     | ROH-hoh              | 🍎    |
| azul         | blue    | ah-SOOL              | 🐋    |
| amarillo     | yellow  | ah-mah-REE-yoh       | 🌞    |
| verde        | green   | VEHR-deh             | 🌳    |
| anaranjado   | orange  | ah-nah-rahn-HAH-doh  | 🦊    |
| morado       | purple  | moh-RAH-doh          | 🍇    |
:::

:::teach {time="7 min"}
**Show-and-say the remaining three colors.** Hold up each card; say the color in Spanish; have students repeat 3 times.

- *"Verde."* (Hold up the green card; gesture toward a plant in the room.)
- *"Anaranjado."* (Pretend to peel an orange.)
- *"Morado."* (Point to something purple, or hold up the purple card.)

Then run a quick **point-and-say** round: hold up a card silently and let kids call out the Spanish word. Catch mispronunciations gently — common slips at this age: *amarillo* gets shortened, *anaranjado* gets confused with *naranja*.
:::

:::practice {time="3 min"}
**"Toca el color"** — *Touch the color*.

Call out a color in Spanish: *"Toca algo **azul**."* Students find and touch something blue in the room (clothing, a crayon, the carpet). First three students to touch correctly each earn a token sticker.

Cycle through all six colors. Keep the pace fast and playful.
:::

:::game {type="bingo" prints="bingo-colores" time="8 min"}
**Bingo de Colores**

1. Hand each student a 3×3 Bingo de Colores card. Each card has six color squares plus three "free" spaces (mascot icons).
2. Teacher calls colors in Spanish one at a time: *"¡Rojo! ... ¡Verde! ... ¡Morado!"*
3. Students cover the matching color square with a token or small marker.
4. First to cover a complete row (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal) shouts *"¡Bingo!"* and earns the day's bonus token.
5. Reset and play one more round if time permits.

*Printable bingo cards are in the binder under "Bingo de Colores" — print one per student.*
:::

:::worksheet {prints="colores-trace-circle" time="4 min"}
**Traza y Colorea** — *Trace and Color*

Hand out the worksheet. Students:

1. Trace the dotted Spanish word at the top of each section (*rojo*, *azul*, *amarillo*, *verde*, *anaranjado*, *morado*).
2. Color the matching picture below with the correct crayon.

This is also a great "early finisher" or homework activity if class is running short.
:::

:::assess
Quick exit check — before students leave their seats, the teacher holds up each color card silently. Each student gives a thumbs-up when they can say the color in Spanish to themselves. (No public quizzing at this age — just self-check.)

Mark mastery in the platform: if a student can confidently name 5/6 colors, tag them complete for this lesson.
:::

:::cultural-note
🇵🇪 *Did you know?* In Peru, the colors of the national flag are **rojo** (red) and **blanco** (white). When you visit Lima, you'll see *banderas rojas y blancas* everywhere on national holidays. (We'll learn *blanco* and *negro* in the next lesson!)
:::

:::extend
For students who finish the worksheet early:

- Find three objects in the room of different colors and whisper their colors to the teacher in Spanish.
- Draw a picture of yourself wearing your favorite color, then label it: *"Yo soy [color]."*
:::

:::teacher-note
**Pronunciation watch:** *Anaranjado* is the longest word and trips up most K4 students. It's fine if they shorten it to *"naranja"* for now — that's also valid Spanish (the word for the fruit, often used colloquially for the color). The full *anaranjado* form is the formal/standard one; we introduce it here so they recognize it later.

**Differentiation:** If you have a heritage speaker in the group, invite them to share which color word their family uses at home (some families say *"colorado"* instead of *"rojo"* in some regions of Mexico, for example). Frame this as "Spanish has many dialects, and they're all correct."

**Next lesson:** *"Blanco y Negro"* — adding black and white. Then on to clothing in lesson 3, which builds on color vocabulary.
:::
