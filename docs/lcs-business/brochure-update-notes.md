## Decisions (locked, action when ready)

### 1. Two separate brochures

The website carries both audiences (schools + general public). The
**printed brochure splits in two**:

- `program-brochure.md` (existing) — stays focused on **schools**.
  Decision-maker: principal, curriculum director, district language
  coordinator. Pitches K4–8 program, ACTFL progression, in-school /
  after-school / summer formats, school-side logistics.
- A second brochure (TBD filename, maybe `community-brochure.md`) —
  for the **broader audience**: adults, retirees, business Spanish,
  homeschool families, private/group 1-on-1. Different reader,
  different pitch, different pricing structure.

### 2. "Peruvian + native fluency" phrasing

Replaces the bare "native speakers" framing throughout the brochures.
The wording:

- Karen and Cece are **"Peruvians with native Spanish fluency"** —
  identity (fact-based) + skill (skill-based).
- Hiring policy for future instructors: **"native-or-near-native
  Spanish fluency at the ACTFL Superior level, evaluated through
  interview and teaching demonstration"** — legally defensible while
  selecting for the same caliber of teacher.

This lets Karen and Cece keep authentic identity language while
protecting LCS LLC legally when hiring future instructors.

### 3. Stats-based positioning, not defensive

The brochure should NOT lead with "Futura / conventional programs are
bad." It should lead with **factual proficiency outcomes** from
credible sources, and let the contrast emerge.

Citable sources to pull from (research pending — do NOT publish stats
without verifying these specific figures):

- **ACTFL** — proficiency benchmark research; outcome studies
  correlating exposure hours to proficiency levels
- **CAL (Center for Applied Linguistics)** — FLES (Foreign Language in
  Elementary School) program longitudinal outcomes
- **CARLA (UMN)** — STARTALK and immersion outcome data
- Possibly **Glossika** or **Pimsleur** institutional research for
  hour-counts-to-conversational-fluency

Target stats to source:
- Typical FLES program (45–90 min/week, K–6): exit proficiency level?
  (Anecdotally Novice Mid, needs verification.)
- Immersion programs (50%+ instructional time in target language):
  exit proficiency?
- Hours of structured instruction typically required to reach ACTFL
  Intermediate Mid?

Until those numbers are in hand with citations, the brochure stays
qualitative ("students typically finish K–8 programs at Novice level")
rather than quantitative.

### 4. School-contract pricing — slight premium over Futura

Futura currently charges Cece's local school **$52/hr** for the
in-school program, with teachers receiving **$26/hr** (50/50 split).

**Karen's call (2026-05-29):** LCS can position **at a slight premium
above $52/hr**, justified by the fluency-first curriculum +
native-fluent Peruvian instructors. Specific number TBD; the
defensible range is roughly **$55–65/hr** (a 6–25% premium). Lower end
is "barely premium, hard for schools to refuse"; higher end is
"meaningful premium, requires the pitch to land hard."

Implication for the brochure's pricing section: the school-paid
"quote-driven" line needs to become more specific. Likely landing at
something like *"Typical school contract rates: $55–65 per hour
depending on class count and group size. Customized proposal sent
after your first meeting with us."*

### 5. Revenue-share split for school contracts

The current `founding-instructors-discussion.md` uses 80/20
(instructor-favored) for direct-pay students. Futura's 50/50 on school
contracts is industry-typical because the LLC does the sales,
contracts, and admin work for school deals.

**Implication:** the founding-instructors doc needs a third row in the
revenue-split table:

| Source | Instructor share | LCS share |
|---|---|---|
| Student you bring (existing relationship) | 80% | 20% |
| Student LCS sources (marketing, SEO) | 60% | 40% |
| **School contract (LCS-sourced, school-paid)** | **50%** | **50%** |

This needs Karen and Cece's sign-off before the founding-teacher
agreements are written. Worth flagging that this is **not a
take-it-away** from teachers — under a $52/hr school deal, the 50/50
split gives the teacher $26/hr for the class itself, plus their share
of any back-end (prep, planning) covered by the LLC.
